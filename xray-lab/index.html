kVp ↑ more ↑ harder ↓ lower ↑ more — ↑ higher Each electron carries more energy; output rises roughly with kVp² and penetration improves.
mA ↑ more — — ↑ more — ↑ higher More electrons per second, so more photons of the same energies. Quantity only.
Exposure time ↑ more — — ↑ more ↓ motion blur ↑ higher Longer collection time at the same mA; identical to mA for dose and mAs, but motion worsens.
Added filtration ↓ fewer ↑ harder ↓ slightly ↑ for equal image — ↓ skin dose Aluminium absorbs soft photons that would only contribute dose, so the beam hardens.
Target atomic number ↑ more ↑ characteristic E — ↓ less heat — — Bremsstrahlung yield scales with Z, and K-lines move to a higher, target-specific energy.
Anode angle — — — — ↓ larger focus — A steeper target projects a smaller focal spot but limits field coverage and strengthens the heel effect.
Focal spot size — — — ↓ per area ↓ blurrier — A larger focus spreads heat but widens geometric unsharpness, especially with magnification.
Source–image distance ↓ fewer — — — ↑ sharper ↓ lower Inverse-square law: doubling distance quarters the intensity, but magnification and blur fall.
Object–image distance — — — — ↓ blurrier ↑ to compensate Magnification grows with OID, amplifying the focal-spot penumbra at object edges.

Dose entries refer to the patient entrance skin dose for a fixed image receptor exposure; automatic exposure control would raise mAs to compensate for filtration or distance.

X-Ray Tube Lab — a transparent teaching model. Spectra are synthesised from a Kramers-style bremsstrahlung continuum with Gaussian characteristic lines; attenuation uses tabulated coefficients. Output is calibrated to 0.45 mGy at 80 kVp / 10 mAs / 2.5 mm Al / 1 m.

Simplifications: monoenergetic contrast index, no scatter, no patient attenuation, ideal detector. Educational use only — not a medical device.
